Create a **3–4 minute traditional Indian musical score for a Tholu Bommalata shadow-puppet animation** telling the origin of **Adrija**, a young girl whose peaceful village life ends in tragedy and awakens her desire for revenge.

**Style:** Authentic **Karnataka folk + Tholu Bommalata theatre + Indian devotional storytelling**. Ancient, spiritual, earthy, emotional and dramatic. Traditional acoustic instruments only: **maddale, mridangam, tavil, ghatam, kanjira, bamboo flute, nadaswaram, veena, tanpura, temple bells and folk cymbals**. No synths, EDM, Bollywood, Western orchestra or modern pop.

**0:00–0:35 — Village & Land**  
Peaceful morning in a Karnataka village. Farmers working the fields, cattle, villagers praying. Soft tanpura, bamboo flute, gentle veena, light percussion and distant temple bells. Warm, innocent and spiritual.

**0:35–0:55 — Birth of Adrija**  
A beautiful sacred melody. Soft flute, veena and bells. The village celebrates the birth of a baby girl. Add subtle female devotional humming, creating a feeling that the child has a special destiny.

**0:55–1:30 — Growing & Training**  
Gradually introduce stronger maddale and mridangam rhythms. Adrija grows into a disciplined warrior. Rhythmic percussion and flute suggest sword practice, running, climbing and physical training. Determined and powerful, but still rooted in folk tradition.

**1:30–1:55 — Enemy Plans the Attack**  
Music suddenly becomes darker. Deep maddale and tavil strikes, ominous nadaswaram and darker drone. Slow, tense rhythm with pauses, creating the feeling that something terrible is approaching.

**1:55–2:35 — Village Attack**  
Explosive traditional percussion. Fast drums, kanjira, cymbals and urgent nadaswaram. The village is attacked; people run, families panic, warriors fight and homes burn. Chaotic, intense and tragic, but still authentic South Indian folk theatre.

**2:35–3:00 — Father Dies**  
Almost all percussion disappears. Low tanpura, slow bamboo flute, sorrowful veena and a distant temple bell. Adrija discovers her father has died. Create deep grief, shock and silence. Use soft female wordless crying.

**3:00–3:35 — Anger Awakens**  
A slow heartbeat-like maddale begins. Dark flute and gradually increasing percussion represent grief becoming controlled anger. Sparse female folk chanting: **“Ha… Ri… Ha…”** and **“Ho… Mahadeva…”** Keep it spiritual and ancient.

**3:35–End — Revenge Begins**  
Build into a powerful heroic climax with traditional drums, nadaswaram and temple bells. Adrija picks up her sword and prepares for revenge. End with one huge drum strike, a sustained temple bell and a deep female chant.

**Emotion:** Peace → Hope → Wonder → Training → Threat → War → Grief → Anger → Revenge → Heroic determination.

The entire score should feel like **one continuous live Tholu Bommalata folk performance**, deeply Indian, spiritual and emotional, with no modern cinematic or electronic sound.
